Why should I choose bonds over fixed deposits?

Both bonds and fixed deposits (FDs) are considered relatively safer investment options, but they differ in terms of returns, liquidity, and tax treatment.

Here are a few points to consider:
  • Certain bonds may offer higher coupon rates than traditional FDs, depending on the issuer, credit rating, and tenure.
  • Many listed bonds can be traded in the secondary market, giving you an opportunity to exit before maturity.
  • While interest on most bonds is taxable (like FDs), certain notified bonds such as Section 54EC Capital Gains Bonds and tax-free PSU bonds may offer specific tax benefits under prevailing income tax laws.

Disclaimer: Bonds carry varying degrees of credit and market risk depending on the issuer and rating. Returns and liquidity are not guaranteed and may differ from traditional FDs. Investors are advised to assess their risk appetite and consult their financial or tax advisor before investing.

Why is it important to deposit in fixed-income assets in 2026?

Fixed-income assets provide capital stability and predictable cash flows, counterbalancing the high volatility of equity markets. They serve as a reliable foundation for preserving wealth and generating regular income over time.

Why It Matters

Allocating to instruments like corporate bonds, debentures, and fixed deposits enables effective portfolio diversification. Unlike market-linked equities, these instruments offer predictable yields, making them ideal for goal-based financial planning such as funding higher education, home down payments, or retirement. To balance risk, SEBI-registered advisors generally suggest scaling your fixed-income allocation based on your age and risk tolerance. This structured approach helps safeguard capital while earning steady returns that depend on the issuer's credit profile and prevailing market conditions.

As an Individual, What is the Need for You to Invest?

Investing means using a part of your money to buy assets, so that your money can grow over time. But why is it needed?
The money you earn from work usually has a limit. A salary can rise only up to a point, the hours you can work each week are fixed, and a business can take in only so much capital. Because of this, your income tends to stay more or less the same over time.
Your expenses, however, do not stay fixed. As you move forward in life, your responsibilities grow. You may need to buy a home, pay for your children's education, handle a medical emergency, or plan for retirement. All of these cost money, and often a large amount.
This is why investing is important. It gives you a second source of income that you can depend on. It also helps you set aside money on a regular basis. Over time, this builds strong financial habits.

What are the Different Types of Investments?

There are many types of investments. But most of them fall into two main groups: Active Investments and Passive Investments.
Active Investments need your time and effort. You must understand how the market works and stay closely involved. Passive Investments work in a different way. Here, you invest your money without being involved every day. The most common method is "buy and hold." You buy the asset and keep it for a fixed period to earn the expected returns.
Bonds and Debentures are good examples of safe and passive investments. You can invest in them and allow your money to grow over time, without watching the market daily.

What are Bonds and Debentures?

Bonds are issued by government bodies. Debentures are issued by private companies, public sector units, banks, and NBFCs. Both are used to raise money. When you buy a Bond or a Debenture, you are lending money to the issuer. In return, the issuer pays you interest and returns your money on a fixed date. In simple terms, Bonds work much like loans. Both have a fixed maturity date. Based on the terms set at the time of issue, you earn interest at regular intervals.
