FAQs about investing in Sovereign Gold Bonds

What are Sovereign Gold Bonds?

Sovereign Gold Bonds are government securities denominated in grams of gold that are issued by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on behalf of the government. These bonds offer an alternative to holding physical gold by allowing investors to buy and hold gold in a digital form.

What are the benefits of investing in SGB over physical gold?

SGBs offer several benefits over physical gold, such as no storage cost, no making charges, and no purity issues. Additionally, SGBs offer a fixed interest rate of 2.5% per annum on the initial investment amount, providing an additional source of income for investors.

Who is eligible to invest in the SGBS?

Person’s resident & Citizen of India are eligible to invest in SGB. Eligible investors include individuals, HUFS, trusts, universities, charitable institutions, etc.

Are there any risks in investing in SGBs?

There may be a risk of capital loss if the market price of gold declines. However, the investor does not lose in terms of the units of gold which he has paid for.

What is the minimum and maximum investment in Sovereign Gold Bonds?

The minimum investment in Sovereign Gold Bonds is 1 gram of gold, and the maximum investment limit for individuals and Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs) is 4 kg per financial year. For trusts and similar entities, the maximum investment limit is 20 kg per fiscal year.

What is the tenure of Sovereign Gold Bonds?

The tenure of Sovereign Gold Bonds is 8 years, but investors have the option to exit after 5 year. The bonds are also tradable on stock exchanges, allowing investors to exit before maturity.
